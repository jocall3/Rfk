# Question 75: How do we stop corporate wellness programs from punishing employees based on body weight?

## The Problem: The Corporate Scale of Shame
Currently, corporate wellness programs act like a high-school bully with a clipboard. They dangle "premium discounts" like a carrot, only to beat you with the stick of higher insurance premiums if your BMI doesn't fit their arbitrary, soul-crushing spreadsheet. It’s not about health; it’s about actuarial bean-counting that treats human beings like depreciating assets. 

RFK Jr. knows that true health isn't found in a corporate-mandated weigh-in. We need to stop the "Biometric Inquisition" before it turns every office breakroom into a scene from a dystopian fitness cult.

## The MAHA Solution: Biometric Sovereignty Amendments
To stop the madness, we are proposing a series of aggressive amendments to the HIPAA wellness provisions. We aren't just tweaking the rules; we are flipping the script to ensure your body data belongs to *you*, not your HR department.

### 1. The "None of Your Business" Clause
We are amending HIPAA to explicitly prohibit the use of body weight, BMI, or body fat percentage as a metric for determining insurance premiums or eligibility for wellness incentives. If a company wants to track "wellness," they can track participation in activities, not the biological outcome of your metabolism.

### 2. Mandatory Data Siloing
Under the new framework, any biometric data collected by a third-party wellness vendor is legally barred from being shared with the employer in a personally identifiable format. The employer gets an aggregate report of "participation," and that’s it. If they try to peek at your individual file, the penalties will be severe enough to make their legal department weep.

### 3. The "Anti-Punishment" Protection
We are codifying a "Right to Opt-Out" without financial penalty. If an employee chooses not to participate in a biometric screening, the employer is legally prohibited from increasing their premiums or reducing their benefits. No more "wellness tax" for people who value their privacy more than a $20 gift card to a smoothie shop.
