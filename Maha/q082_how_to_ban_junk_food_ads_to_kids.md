## The Policy Blueprint: FTC Trade Regulation Rules On Algorithmic UPF Marketing

How do we legally crush the digital sugar pipeline? We don't rely on "voluntary industry guidelines" (which are about as effective as asking a raccoon to voluntarily guard a trash can). We use the administrative hammer of the Federal Trade Commission Act.

### 1. Promulgating Trade Regulation Rules Under FTC Section 6(g) and Section 18
Under Section 5 of the FTC Act (15 U.S.C. § 45), the FTC has the authority to prohibit "unfair or deceptive acts or practices." We will initiate a formal Trade Regulation Rulemaking process targeting the digital marketing of Ultra-Processed Foods to minors under age 18.

* **Defining UPFs Legally:** Using the NOVA Classification System (Group 4) as our baseline, defined by industrial formulations containing little to no whole food, packed with hydrogenated oils, high-fructose corn syrup, flavor enhancers, and artificial emulsifiers.
* **The "Unfairness" Finding:** Targeting children whose prefrontal cortexes are still under construction with hyper-palatable food cues designed by behavioral scientists is *per se* unfair under Section 5(n). Minors lack the cognitive capability to resist algorithmic neuro-marketing.

### 2. Complete Ban on Algorithmic Micro-Targeting & Behavioral Profiling
It will be illegal for any digital platform (YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Roblox, Twitch, mobile games) to use machine-learning algorithms to serve UPF advertisements to users known or predicted to be under 18 based on:
* Behavioral watch history and engagement data.
* Location-based contextual triggers (e.g., serving a fast-food ad when a teen walks within 500 feet of a drive-thru).
* Biometric, emotion-tracking, or eye-tracking data embedded in app software.

### 3. Modernizing COPPA (COPPA 2.0 Enforcement Integration)
We are updating enforcement protocols under the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA):
* **Eliminate the "Actual Knowledge" Loophole:** Platforms can no longer pretend they "didn't know" an 11-year-old was using their app just because the kid checked a box claiming to be born in 1904.
* **Prohibit Surveillance Advertising:** No custom profile building, no cookie-retargeting, and no lookalike audience modeling targeted at minors for food and beverage categories.

### 4. Algorithmic Transparency and Audit Requirements
Big Tech loves to hide behind their proprietary "black box." Not anymore.
* **Mandatory Code Audits:** The FTC's Bureau of Consumer Protection will establish a specialized **Algorithmic Auditing Unit**. Tech firms must open their recommendation engines and ad-placement neural networks for quarterly compliance reviews.
* **Disgorgement of Profits:** If a tech platform or food company is caught illegally serving algorithmic sugar ads to kids, the FTC will enforce mandatory disgorgement of *all revenue* generated from that ad campaign, plus statutory fines of up to **$50,120 per violation per user**. (Watch how fast YouTube fixes its ad engine when a single bad cereal campaign costs them $4 billion).
