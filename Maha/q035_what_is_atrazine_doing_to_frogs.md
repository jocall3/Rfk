# Question 35: What is atrazine doing to frogs, and why should humans be worried?

## The Amphibian Identity Crisis
So, let’s talk about atrazine. It’s a popular herbicide, the kind of stuff that makes cornfields look pristine and weed-free. It’s also, apparently, the world’s most aggressive gender-bending chemical. 

If you’re a frog living in a pond downstream from a farm, you’re having a really bad time. Research has shown that atrazine acts as an endocrine disruptor, essentially turning male frogs into females. We’re talking about gonadal dysgenesis—where frogs end up with ovaries where their testes should be. It’s a biological comedy of errors, except the punchline is that the frogs can’t reproduce, and the population is collapsing. 

## Why Should Humans Care?
"But I’m not a frog!" you might say. "I don't live in a pond!" 

Well, congratulations on your land-dwelling status, but atrazine is a common groundwater contaminant. It’s in the water supply. It’s in the runoff. It’s everywhere. If a chemical can fundamentally scramble the hormonal signaling of a vertebrate, do we really think it’s just sitting there politely ignoring our own endocrine systems? 

We are essentially running a massive, uncontrolled experiment on the American population, and the control group is currently a bunch of confused frogs.
