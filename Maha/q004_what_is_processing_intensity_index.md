# Question 4: What is the Processing Intensity Index (PII)?

The Processing Intensity Index (PII) is our proprietary, highly scientific, and frankly terrifying metric used to quantify exactly how much a food product has been "tortured" before it hits your plate. 

Think of it as a "Misery Score" for your dinner. 

### How It Works
We track the journey of a single corn kernel from the field to the bag. If that kernel was bleached, pulverized, extruded through a high-pressure nozzle, dyed with petroleum-based chemicals, and reconstituted into a uniform, sad-looking triangle, the PII score goes up. 

A raw apple has a PII of 0. A neon-orange, "cheese-flavored" corn puff? That’s hitting triple digits. We measure the number of industrial interventions required to turn a natural ingredient into a shelf-stable, nutrient-void science experiment.

### Why the Processed Food Lobby Hates This
The lobby wants you to think "food is food." They spend millions on marketing to convince you that their "Flavor-Blasted" snacks are just corn and seasoning. The PII exposes the truth: these products are essentially industrial polymers masquerading as sustenance. 

When we publish these scores, the lobbyists start sweating. They realize that if people actually understood that their "chips" were reconstituted in a factory that looks more like a chemical refinery than a kitchen, they might actually choose to eat something that didn't require a PhD in chemical engineering to manufacture.
