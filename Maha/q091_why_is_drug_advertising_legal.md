# Question 91: Why is the US one of only two countries that allows pharmaceutical ads on television?

## The Status Quo: A Nation of Pill-Popping Spectators
The United States and New Zealand are the only two countries on Earth that allow Direct-to-Consumer Pharmaceutical Advertising (DTCPA). It is a bizarre, dystopian reality where you are trying to enjoy a nice dinner, and suddenly a commercial tells you that your "sadness" is actually a chemical imbalance that can only be fixed by a pill with 47 side effects, including "spontaneous combustion" and "an uncontrollable urge to yodel."

It’s a multi-billion dollar industry designed to turn patients into customers and doctors into glorified vending machines. The pharmaceutical lobby spends more on advertising than they do on actual research, ensuring that every American knows the name of a drug they don't need before they know how to eat a vegetable.

## The MAHA Strategy: Ending the Corporate Subsidy of Sickness
Under the MAHA (Make America Healthy Again) framework, we are ending the party. Currently, pharmaceutical companies get to write off their massive advertising budgets as a "business expense," effectively forcing the American taxpayer to subsidize the very commercials that make us sick.

1. **Elimination of Tax Deductibility:** We are stripping the tax-deductible status from all DTCPA. If Big Pharma wants to spend billions telling you that your life is incomplete without their latest injectable, they can pay for it with their own profits—not with your tax dollars.
2. **The "Equal Time" Mandate:** If a pharmaceutical company insists on airing a commercial for a drug, they must—by federal mandate—fund an equal amount of airtime for a "Lifestyle Intervention" segment. This segment will feature actual health experts explaining how to fix the underlying condition through nutrition, exercise, and sunlight.
