# Question 87: Why is it so hard to get your own medical records?

## The Current State of Medical Gatekeeping
Right now, getting your own medical records is like trying to break into Fort Knox with a library card. You’d think your health data belongs to you, right? Wrong. It’s currently trapped in a digital purgatory of proprietary software, fax machines from 1994, and administrative "red tape" designed to make you give up and just go back to eating your processed sludge.

The medical establishment treats your data like a state secret. They want you to stay sick, stay confused, and stay dependent. They don't want you to have your records because if you actually knew what was going on, you might start asking questions—and we can't have that, can we?

## The MAHA Solution: Absolute Ownership
RFK Jr. is coming for the gatekeepers. We aren't just asking for access; we are demanding absolute, sovereign ownership of your biological data. 

### The 21st Century Cures Act Upgrade
We are taking the existing 21st Century Cures Act and putting it on steroids. Here is the plan:

1.  **Absolute Individual Ownership:** Your medical history is your property. Period. If a hospital tries to withhold your data, they aren't just being "difficult"—they are committing a violation of your personal property rights.
2.  **Instant Digital Portability:** We are mandating a universal, open-source API standard. Your records should be as easy to move as a Spotify playlist. You want to switch doctors? Click a button. You want to upload your blood work to a health app that actually cares about your longevity? Done.
3.  **The "Fax Machine Ban":** Any healthcare provider caught using a fax machine to delay the transfer of patient records will be publicly shamed and fined into oblivion. It’s time to join the 21st century.
