## The Origin Story: How Earl Butz Ruined the American Waistline

To understand why we are drowning in liquid diabetes, we have to travel back to the early 1970s. Meet **Earl Butz**, Richard Nixon’s Secretary of Agriculture. 

Before Butz, US agricultural policy was focused on managing supply to keep prices stable for farmers. If there was too much corn, the government paid farmers *not* to plant, preventing prices from crashing. But in the early '70s, food prices spiked, Nixon got nervous about his re-election, and he told Earl Butz to fix it.

Butz’s solution was simple, brutal, and catastrophic: **"Get big or get out."** 

He told farmers to plant **"fence row to fence row."** He dismantled the old supply-management systems and replaced them with a system of direct subsidy payments. The goal? Produce as much corn as humanly possible, no matter the cost, no matter the quality, and let the market sort it out.

### The Great Corn Tsunami
American farmers did exactly what they were told. They planted. And planted. And planted. Soon, the US was buried under a literal mountain of surplus corn. 

Now, you can't just eat raw field corn (yellow dent corn #2) off the cob. It tastes like chalky cardboard. You have to process it. So, the food industry had to figure out what to do with billions of bushels of cheap, government-subsidized corn. 

Two beautiful disasters emerged from this surplus:
1. **Factory-farmed, grain-fed beef:** We started stuffing cows (which are evolutionarily designed to eat grass) full of corn, ruining their microbiomes and altering the fat profile of our meat.
2. **High-Fructose Corn Syrup (HFCS):** In the late 1970s, Japanese researchers perfected the industrial process to turn corn starch into a super-sweet liquid. Because the US government was heavily subsidizing corn *and* placing high tariffs on imported real sugar, HFCS became dirt cheap. 

It was the perfect storm of corporate greed: the government paid farmers to grow the corn, factories turned it into cheap liquid sugar, and food conglomerates pumped it into every processed food imaginable to make them hyper-palatable and addictive.

### 1. Price Loss Coverage (PLC) & Agricultural Risk Coverage (ARC)
These programs guarantee that if the price of corn drops below a certain benchmark, the government steps in and pays the farmers the difference. Imagine if you opened a lemonade stand, and the government promised that if nobody bought your lemonade, they would buy all of it from you anyway at a premium. You’d start pouring lemonade down the storm drain just to collect the checks! That is the US corn market.

### 2. Highly Subsidized Crop Insurance
The federal government pays about 60% of the premiums for farmers' crop insurance. This completely eliminates the financial risk of planting massive monocultures of corn year after year, destroying the soil and ignoring crop rotation. If the crop fails, the taxpayer bails them out. If the crop succeeds, we get flooded with cheap syrup. It’s a win-win for big ag, and a lose-lose for American livers.
