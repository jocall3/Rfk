## The Current Circus: How the CDC "Shares" Data

Right now, if an independent researcher, epidemiologist, or Bobby Kennedy wants to look at raw national health data to figure out why childhood autoimmune diseases are skyrocketing, the process goes something like this:

1. You submit a FOIA request.
2. The CDC puts your request in a special filing cabinet located inside a decommissioned salt mine.
3. Three years later, they mail you a 4,000-page scanned PDF.
4. Half the pages are completely blacked out with a Sharpie.
5. The remaining pages are low-resolution JPEGs of tables formatted in Comic Sans from 1998.
6. Good luck running a Python script on a scanned paper copy of a graph, nerd.

The CDC treats public health data like it's the secret formula for Coca-Cola or the coordinates to Atlantis. They curate it, delay it, tweak it, aggregate it until all useful context is scrubbed away, and then issue a press release telling you: *"Trust us, everything is fine, go eat your frosted corn flakes."*

## Enter the Solution: The Public Health Data Freedom Act

We are done begging bureaucratic gatekeepers for scraps of information. The **Public Health Data Freedom Act** turns the CDC’s data vault inside out and dumps it right onto the public internet.

Here is what the Act mandates:

### 1. Raw, Machine-Readable Datasets by Default
No more locked PDFs. No more "summarized reports" where the CDC decides what you're allowed to see. 
* All anonymized individual-level patient data (de-identified per HIPAA standard) must be uploaded in raw, standardized formats (**CSV, JSON, Parquet, or SQL dumps**).
* If a high school kid in Ohio wants to run a machine learning model on national diabetes trends vs. food additive consumption over the last 20 years, they should be able to do it in three clicks.

### 2. Real-Time Public APIs
Public health data delayed by two years isn't "health tracking"—it's an archaeology project. 
* The CDC will be required to maintain high-uptime, open-access REST APIs.
* As soon as state health departments report anonymized data, it hits the public pipeline within **30 days**. 

### 3. Automatic Penalty Clause
What happens when bureaucrats pull the "we need more time to review" excuse?
* If a dataset is withheld past its statutory release deadline without a certified national security clearance, the agency budget for "Executive Communications and Public Relations" drops by **2% per week**.
* Watch how fast those CSVs get uploaded when the CDC's PR budget threatens to hit zero.
