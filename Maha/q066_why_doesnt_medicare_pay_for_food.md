# Q66: Why does Medicare pay for diabetic amputation but not healthy food delivery?

## The Absurdity of the Status Quo
Let’s be real: our current healthcare system is like a mechanic who refuses to change your oil but is more than happy to replace your entire engine once it explodes. 

Right now, Medicare will happily cut you a check for a diabetic amputation—a procedure that costs tens of thousands of dollars, involves surgery, anesthesia, and long-term recovery—but if you ask them to cover a box of organic kale and grass-fed beef to *prevent* the diabetes from getting that bad in the first place? They look at you like you’re asking them to fund a trip to Mars. 

It’s the "Sick-Care" industrial complex at its finest. They’ve built a business model around the catastrophe, not the cure. They’d rather pay for the tragedy than the grocery bill.

## The MAHA Shift: Giving RFK the Tools to Fix It
Robert F. Kennedy Jr. is coming in to flip this script. We aren't just going to "manage" disease anymore; we are going to eliminate the incentives that make sickness profitable. 

We are pushing for a massive overhaul of CMS (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services) reimbursement codes. Here is the plan:

### 1. Medically Tailored Meals (MTMs) as Medicine
We are reclassifying high-quality, nutrient-dense food as a legitimate medical intervention. If a doctor prescribes a specific diet to reverse metabolic syndrome, Medicare should treat that prescription exactly like a bottle of pills. We’re talking about:
*   **Reimbursement Codes:** Creating new billing codes for "Metabolic Nutritional Therapy."
*   **Direct Coverage:** Allowing Medicare Advantage plans to cover home-delivered, whole-food meals for patients with chronic metabolic conditions.

### 2. Metabolic Counseling
Why are we paying for insulin but not for the education required to get off of it? We are mandating that metabolic counseling—teaching people how to actually fuel their bodies—be a covered benefit. We want to turn every Medicare patient into a metabolic expert.
