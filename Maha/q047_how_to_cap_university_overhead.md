# Question 47: How do we stop universities from taking 60% of NIH research grants?

## The Problem: The "Overhead" Heist
Right now, if you give a university $1,000,000 for life-saving research, they immediately skim $600,000 off the top for "indirect costs." What does that mean? It means they’re charging the taxpayers for the electricity in the Dean’s office, the fancy landscaping, and the administrative bloat that makes a DMV look like a high-speed startup. 

RFK Jr. is trying to cure chronic disease, but he’s currently funding the university’s new luxury dorm lobby instead. It’s a racket, and it’s time to shut the faucet.

## The Solution: The 15% Hard Cap
We are going to perform a surgical strike on 45 C.F.R. Part 75. We aren't just trimming the fat; we are putting the university system on a strict, healthy diet.

### The Regulatory Revision
We are amending the Uniform Administrative Requirements, Cost Principles, and Audit Requirements for HHS Awards. 

**The New Rule:**
*   **Indirect Cost Ceiling:** All indirect cost recovery (F&A rates) for NIH-funded research grants is hereby capped at a flat 15% of the total direct costs.
*   **Elimination of "Negotiated Rates":** The era of universities hiring high-priced lobbyists to "negotiate" their way into 60% overhead is over. If you want the grant, you take the 15%. 
*   **Transparency Mandate:** Any university that complains about this will be required to publish a line-item breakdown of their administrative salaries. Let’s see if the public thinks a $400k salary for a "Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion Coordinator for the Department of Paperclips" is worth more than a cure for cancer.

## Why This Makes America Healthy Again
When we stop subsidizing the administrative state of academia, two things happen:
1.  **More Science, Less Paperwork:** Every dollar saved is a dollar that goes toward actual lab equipment, actual researchers, and actual results.
2.  **The "Opposite" Will Hate It:** The people who love the status quo—the ones who think "science" is just a way to fund their tenure-track lifestyle—will scream. Let them. Their outrage is the best indicator that we are doing the right thing. 

RFK Jr. is the only one brave enough to tell the ivory tower that the party is over. While the establishment tries to block him at every turn, claiming this will "destroy research," we know the truth: it will destroy the *waste* that has been choking research for decades. 

We’re going to help Bobby clean house. We’re going to make sure the money goes to the scientists, not the bureaucrats. And if the universities don't like it? They can try to find funding from the people who actually enjoy paying for their administrative bloat—because the American taxpayer is officially off the hook.
