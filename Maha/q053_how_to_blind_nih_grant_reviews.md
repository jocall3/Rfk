# Question 53: How do we make NIH grant reviews double-blind?

## The Problem: The "Prestige Trap"
Right now, the NIH grant review process is basically a high-stakes version of a high school popularity contest. If you’re from an Ivy League school or have a name that sounds like it belongs on a building, you get the money. If you’re a brilliant outsider with a radical idea that could actually cure something, you get laughed out of the room because your CV doesn't have enough "prestige." It’s a closed loop of groupthink, and it’s killing innovation.

## The Solution: Double-Blind Everything
To Make America Healthy Again (MAHA), we need to stop funding the "who" and start funding the "what." We are implementing a strict double-blind review process. 

1. **Identity Scrubbing:** All proposals will be stripped of investigator names, institutional affiliations, and past publication history. If the reviewers can’t see that you’re from Harvard, they have to actually read your science.
2. **The "Idea-First" Protocol:** Reviewers will be presented with the core hypothesis and methodology first. If the science is garbage, it gets tossed immediately. If it’s brilliant, it moves to the next round.
3. **High-Risk/High-Reward Track:** We are creating a dedicated "Moonshot" lane. This is for the wild, crazy, "this might fail but if it works it changes the world" ideas. These proposals are reviewed by a rotating panel of contrarians and outsiders—people who aren't currently sucking at the NIH teat.

## Why the Establishment Hates This
The current gatekeepers are terrified. Why? Because if we remove the "prestige" filter, their entire business model collapses. They don't want to compete on merit; they want to compete on their Rolodex. They’ll tell you this is "unscientific" or "risky." 

Translation: "Please don't take away my guaranteed funding for my 15th study on the same tired topic."
