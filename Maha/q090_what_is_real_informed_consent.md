# Question 90: Why don't doctors tell you the absolute risk reduction of a drug?

## The Great Statistical Shell Game
Let’s be honest: if your doctor told you, "This drug reduces your risk of a heart attack from 0.2% to 0.1%," you’d probably tell them to go jump in a lake. But if they say, "This drug cuts your risk in half!"—well, suddenly you’re reaching for your wallet. 

That, my friends, is the magic of **Relative Risk Reduction (RRR)**. It’s the pharmaceutical industry’s favorite parlor trick. By hiding the **Absolute Risk Reduction (ARR)**, they turn a statistical rounding error into a "medical breakthrough." It’s like saying you’ve doubled your chances of winning the lottery by buying two tickets instead of one. Technically true, but you’re still probably not going to the Bahamas.

## Why the Silence?
Doctors aren't necessarily malicious; they’re just busy, tired, and often as brainwashed by Big Pharma’s glossy brochures as the rest of us. The medical establishment treats ARR like a state secret because if patients actually understood the tiny, microscopic benefits of most blockbuster drugs, the entire "pill for every ill" business model would collapse faster than a soufflé in an earthquake.

## The MAHA Blueprint: Radical Transparency
RFK Jr. is coming to town, and he’s bringing a flashlight to the dark corners of the FDA. To Make America Healthy Again, we are rewriting the rules of engagement. We are expanding the **Common Rule** to mandate that informed consent isn't just a signature on a piece of paper—it’s a genuine understanding of the math.

### The New Disclosure Mandate:
Under the MAHA framework, every prescription and medical intervention must be accompanied by a standardized "Truth-in-Labeling" disclosure:

1.  **The ARR Reality Check:** Doctors must provide the Absolute Risk Reduction in plain English. No more hiding behind percentages of percentages. If the drug prevents one case in 1,000 people, that’s what the label says.
2.  **The Toxicological Profile:** We’re done with "side effects" being buried in fine print. We want a clear, bold-faced list of the toxicological risks, including long-term metabolic and neurological impacts.
3.  **Liability Transparency:** If a manufacturer is shielded from liability, the patient has a right to know. If the company isn't willing to stand behind their product in court, why should you trust them with your bloodstream?
