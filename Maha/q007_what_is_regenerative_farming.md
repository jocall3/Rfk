# Question 7: What is Regenerative Farming?

## The Soil Microbiome vs. The Wall Street Boardroom

Let’s be honest: if you put a Wall Street executive in a room with a handful of healthy topsoil, the soil would probably come out with a better 401(k) and a more coherent long-term strategy. 

Regenerative farming isn't just "farming." It’s the art of stopping the industrial war on our own dinner plates. Right now, Big Ag treats soil like a glorified sponge for chemicals—a lifeless medium designed to hold up "dirt-flavored cardboard" (also known as modern industrial corn). 

Regenerative farming flips the script. It focuses on:
1. **Minimal Soil Disturbance:** Stop tilling the earth like you're trying to bury a secret.
2. **Cover Cropping:** Keeping the ground covered so it doesn't get lonely or eroded.
3. **Biodiversity:** Bringing back the bugs, the worms, and the fungi.
4. **Livestock Integration:** Letting cows do what cows do best—fertilize the land naturally instead of sitting in a concrete feedlot eating GMO soy.

## Why the Soil is Smarter Than You Think
The soil microbiome is a bustling, high-frequency trading floor of nutrients. It’s a complex, intelligent network of bacteria, fungi, and protozoa that trade minerals for sugars with plant roots. It’s a symbiotic economy that actually works—unlike the one in D.C. 

When we spray the land with glyphosate and synthetic fertilizers, we’re essentially nuking the local economy of the soil. We’re killing the "employees" (the microbes) and then wondering why the "product" (our food) has the nutritional density of a wet napkin.
