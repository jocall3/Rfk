### SECTION I: WHAT ON EARTH IS "GRAS" AND HOW DID WE GET HOODWINKED?

To understand this madness, we must travel back to 1958, when President Dwight D. Eisenhower signed the Food Additives Amendment to the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act [13]. The original goal was noble: make sure chemical companies weren't putting literal rat poison into the bread of hard-working Americans [14]. However, Congress realized that some things didn't need rigorous, multi-million-dollar scientific testing, such as vinegar, salt, baking soda, and black pepper [15].

Thus, they created an exemption: if an ingredient was "Generally Recognized As Safe" by qualified experts based on common use in food before 1958, it didn't have to go through the grueling FDA pre-market approval process [16]. It was a common-sense carve-out so food companies didn't have to spend five years proving that oregano wouldn't cause your third eye to open [17].

But then, the corporate lawyers and chemical conglomerates looked at this common-sense exemption and saw a multi-billion-dollar highway to bypass federal oversight entirely [18]. Over the last few decades, this narrow exemption was stretched, warped, and abused until it completely swallowed the entire food regulatory system [19]. Today, over 10,000 chemicals have been introduced into our food supply, and the FDA has thoroughly tested almost none of them for safety [20].
