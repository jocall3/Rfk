# Question 94: Why do drug companies hide clinical trials that failed?

## The "Oops, We Forgot" Strategy
Let’s be honest: if you were selling a "miracle" potion that actually just made people grow extra elbows or feel a sudden, inexplicable urge to bark at mailmen, would you post the results on the front page of the New York Times? Probably not. 

Big Pharma has spent decades treating failed clinical trials like that one embarrassing photo from your middle school dance—tucked away in a dark, dusty drawer, never to be seen by human eyes again. They call it "proprietary data," but we call it "hiding the bodies." When a drug fails to show efficacy or causes side effects that make a horror movie look like a rom-com, the industry’s favorite move is to bury the data so deep that even a team of archaeologists couldn't find it.

## The "MAHA" Fix: Forcing Transparency
RFK Jr. isn't just going to ask nicely for this data; he’s going to make it impossible to hide. We are talking about strict enforcement of the **Public Health Service Act amendments**. 

Here is the new reality for the pharmaceutical giants:
1. **The 90-Day Rule:** If you run a clinical trial, you have 90 days to publish the protocol and the results. Not 91 days. Not "when we feel like it." 90 days.
2. **No More "Ghost" Trials:** If it happened, it gets published. Period. If you try to hide a failed trial, the penalties will be so severe that your shareholders will start sweating through their silk suits.
3. **Public Accountability:** We are creating a centralized, searchable database where every single trial—successful or a total dumpster fire—is available for the American people to see. 

## Why the Opposition is Sweating
The status quo loves the shadows. They thrive on the "trust us, we’re scientists" narrative while keeping the actual science under lock and key. By forcing the publication of all trial results, we are effectively turning on the stadium lights in a dark alley. 

The opposition is terrified because they know that once the public sees the sheer volume of "failed" experiments that were swept under the rug, the jig is up. They don't want you to be healthy; they want you to be a recurring subscription model.
