# Question 51: How do we kick pharmaceutical and food executives off NIH grant review boards?

## The Problem: The Fox is Guarding the Henhouse (and Selling the Eggs)
Currently, the NIH peer-review process is so cozy with industry that it’s basically a corporate networking event with a government budget. We have executives from Big Pharma and Big Food sitting on grant review boards, deciding which research gets funded. It’s like asking a vampire to chair a committee on blood bank safety. They aren't looking for cures; they’re looking for "market-aligned outcomes."

## The MAHA Strategy: The "No-Conflict" Purge
To Make America Healthy Again, we need to perform a total systemic cleanse. We are going to implement a "Zero-Industry-Influence" policy for all NIH grant review panels.

### 1. The "Clean Hands" Mandate
We are implementing an immediate, non-negotiable ban on any individual serving on an NIH review board if they have held a position at a pharmaceutical, food, or chemical corporation within the last 10 years. If you’ve spent a decade pushing ultra-processed sludge or patent-protected pills, you don’t get to decide what science is "valid."

### 2. Financial Tie Screenings (The "Follow the Money" Audit)
We are deploying an aggressive, AI-driven financial audit of every panel member. 
*   **Stock Holdings:** If you own stock in a company that benefits from the research you’re reviewing, you’re out. 
*   **Consulting Fees:** If you’ve taken a "speaking fee" or "consulting retainer" from an industry player, you’re out. 
*   **Spousal/Family Ties:** We’re looking at the whole household. No more "my wife works for Pfizer, but I’m totally objective" nonsense.

### 3. The "Public Interest" Requirement
Every panel member must sign a legally binding affidavit stating that their primary loyalty is to the health of the American public, not the quarterly earnings of a conglomerate. If they lie, they face federal perjury charges. We’re making it so scary to be a corporate shill in government that they’ll run back to their boardrooms voluntarily.
