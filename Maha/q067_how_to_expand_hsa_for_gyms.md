# Question 67: How do we let Americans use pre-tax HSA money for gym memberships and organic food?

## The Problem: The "Sick-Care" Tax Trap
Currently, the IRS treats your Health Savings Account (HSA) like a high-security vault that only opens if you’re already sick. You can buy a box of bandages, but heaven forbid you buy a gym membership to prevent the injury in the first place. It’s like paying for a fire extinguisher only after your house has already burned down. 

The establishment loves this. They want you sedentary, eating processed sludge, and waiting until you’re a "customer" of the pharmaceutical-industrial complex. They don't want you healthy; they want you billable.

## The Solution: Amending Section 223 of the Internal Revenue Code
We are going to drag the IRS into the 21st century. We need to amend Section 223 of the Internal Revenue Code to redefine "qualified medical expenses" to include proactive health investments.

### 1. The "Gym-is-Medicine" Amendment
We will classify gym memberships, personal training sessions, and home fitness equipment as "preventative medical care." If you’re sweating, you’re saving the system money. The IRS should be subsidizing your deadlifts, not your diabetes medication.

### 2. The "Real Food" Rebate
We will expand HSA eligibility to include certified organic, non-processed whole foods. If the government can subsidize corn syrup, we can certainly let you use your own pre-tax money to buy kale. We’ll implement a simple verification system at the point of sale—if it’s grown in dirt and not a lab, it’s a tax-deductible health investment.
