# Revolving Door Registry: Government and Industry Personnel Transitions

## Executive Summary

The "revolving door" refers to the movement of high-level personnel between public sector regulatory/policy roles and private sector corporate/lobbying positions. This registry tracks key figures across federal regulatory agencies—specifically the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), Federal Communications Commission (FCC), and Department of Defense (DOD)—documenting their career trajectories, policy decisions, and corporate affiliations.

## Systematic Patterns Identified in Revolving Door Dynamics

1. **Regulatory Capture & Post-Employment Compensation:** Officials frequently secure lucrative corporate board positions within months of departing federal agencies, benefiting from regulatory networks established during public tenure.
2. **Pre-Service Lobbying Influence:** Appointees entering government directly from lobbying or executive roles often oversee decisions affecting former employers or client networks.
3. **Cooling-Off Period Circumvention:** Strategic reliance on "strategic consulting" or non-registered advisory roles allows individuals to bypass formal federal lobbying restrictions.
4. **Bi-Directional Reciprocity:** Bilateral movement creates shared institutional norms, risk tolerances, and evaluation metrics between private industry and public regulatory bodies.
