### PART I: THE ABSURDITY OF THE "GRADE YOUR OWN HOMEWORK" SYSTEM

To understand how we got here, we have to look at the Food Additives Amendment of 1958, signed by President Eisenhower [^13]. Back then, Congress decided that common-sense ingredients like vinegar, table salt, and black pepper did not need five years of clinical trials before being sprinkled on a pork chop [^14]. They called these ingredients "Generally Recognized As Safe" (GRAS) [^15]. 

However, corporate food lobbyists looked at this common-sense exemption and saw a loophole wider than a supertanker full of high-fructose corn syrup [^16]. Over the next several decades, the FDA allowed this exemption to swallow the entire food safety law [^17]. Under a voluntary notification rule established in 1997, the FDA essentially told the food industry, "If you think a chemical is safe, just go ahead and put it in the food; you don't even have to tell us" [^18]. 

This created a system of "Secret GRAS" [^19]. If a chemical company invents a new synthetic emulsifier—let's call it *Mega-Glaze-9000*, which doubles as an industrial engine degreaser—they do not send it to the FDA for approval [^20]. Instead, they hire their own private panel of scientists, pay them handsome consulting fees, and ask them to sign a piece of paper declaring it safe [^21]. The paid-off scientists rubber-stamp the chemical, the company slips it into your kid's toaster pastries, and the FDA literally has no idea the chemical even exists [^22]. 

According to the Environmental Working Group, food and chemical companies have rubber-stamped at least 111 food chemicals for use in thousands of products without ever notifying the FDA [^23]. In fact, since the year 2000, nearly 99% of new food chemicals entered the market through this exact loophole [^24]. It is a protection racket run by guys in white lab coats funded by guys in tailored suits, and it has turned the American food supply into a toxic science experiment [^25].
