### ACT I: THE STEAMSHIP LUBRICANT OF THE HIGH SEAS

Before it was drizzled over your "healthy" organic kale chips, canola oil had a much more honest job: **keeping the gears of World War II naval destroyers from grinding to a screeching halt.**

The plant is *Brassica napus*, commonly known as **Rapeseed**. 

Why was rapeseed oil so beloved by the Royal Canadian Navy? Because it possessed a unique physical property: it adhered to wet metal surfaces better than almost any other substance on Earth. When steamships were chugging across the Atlantic, dodging German U-boats, their massive engines needed a heavy-duty, high-erucic-acid lubricant that wouldn't wash off in the presence of superheated steam and salt water. 

Rapeseed oil was the ultimate industrial lube. 

But then, the war ended. The steamships were decommissioned. The Canadian agricultural sector suddenly found themselves with millions of acres of yellow-flowered rapeseed crops and absolutely no military contracts.

### ACT II: THE GREAT CANADIAN HEIST (THE BIRTH OF "CANOLA")

You can't sell something called "Rapeseed Oil" in the grocery store. It’s a marketing nightmare on multiple levels. Furthermore, the FDA actually banned rapeseed oil for human consumption in 1956 because of its cardiotoxic properties (specifically, the erucic acid).

In the 1970s, Canadian plant breeders used traditional selective breeding (and later, heavy genetic modification) to create a variant of rapeseed that was low in erucic acid and low in glucosinolates (the compounds that made it taste like bitter garbage).

They called this new creation **LEAR** (Low Erucic Acid Rapeseed). 

But "LEAR" still sounded like a villainous organization from a comic book. They needed a name that sounded clean, wholesome, and completely divorced from its industrial, steamship-lubricating past.

They took **CAN** (Canada) + **O** (Oil) + **L** (Low) + **A** (Acid) and created: **CANOLA**.

### ACT III: THE CHEMICAL TORTURE CHAMBER (HOW IT'S MADE)

If you want to make olive oil, you squeeze an olive. If you want to make butter, you churn cream. It’s beautiful. It’s natural. You could do it in your kitchen.

If you want to make Canola Oil, you need a multi-million-dollar chemical refinery and a hazmat suit. 

Here is the actual, horrifying process of how this "heart-healthy" oil is extracted from the rapeseed:

1. **The Crush:** The seeds are crushed under immense mechanical pressure. This generates high heat, which immediately oxidizes the fragile polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs), turning them rancid before they even leave the seed.
2. **The Hexane Bath:** Mechanical pressing only gets some of the oil out. To squeeze out every last drop of profit, the seed cake is bathed in **Hexane**—a toxic solvent derived from crude oil that is also used in varnishes and quick-drying glues. 
3. **The Acid Wash:** The crude oil is then treated with sodium hydroxide (lye) and phosphoric acid to remove gums and impurities.
4. **The Bleaching:** At this point, the oil is a dark, foul-smelling, muddy sludge. It is filtered through acid-activated clays to bleach out the horrifying color.
5. **The Deodorization:** Because the oil has been subjected to extreme heat and chemical solvents, it smells absolutely rancid. To make it palatable, it is subjected to steam distillation at temperatures up to **500°F (260°C)** under a vacuum. This high-heat process destroys any remaining nutrients and converts a significant portion of the omega-3 fatty acids into highly toxic **trans-fats**—which are then conveniently left off the nutrition label.
6. **The Preservatives:** To keep this chemically mutilated oil from spoiling on the shelf, synthetic antioxidants like BHA, BHT, or TBHQ are added.

You are not eating food. You are eating a highly processed, chemically deodorized, industrial byproduct that has been bleached and scrubbed so your tongue doesn't immediately recognize it as poison.
