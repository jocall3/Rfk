## 3. The Legal Weaponry: Forcing the FDA to Remember that Zero Means Zero

### The Delaney Clause (The Law They Tried to Kill)
Passed as part of the Food Additives Amendment of 1958, the Delaney Clause is beautifully, brutally simple:

> *"The Secretary of Food and Drugs shall not approve for use in food any chemical additive if it is found to induce cancer when ingested by man or animal, or if it is found, after tests which are appropriate for the evaluation of the safety of food additives, to induce cancer in man or animal."*

Notice what it *doesn't* say? It doesn't say "unless it's just a little bit of cancer." It doesn't say "unless the food lobby gives us a really nice vacation in Cabo." It says **SHALL NOT APPROVE**.

### How the Bureaucrats Bypassed It
In the late 1980s, the FDA tried to read a "de minimis" exception into the Delaney Clause. The courts actually slapped them down in *Les v. Reilly* (1992), ruling that the statute was clear: **zero means zero**. 

But the FDA, being the slippery eels they are, bypassed this by shifting chemicals into the "Generally Recognized as Safe" (GRAS) category, where the Delaney Clause is ignored, or by using "risk assessment" models that assume humans are made of indestructible plastic.
