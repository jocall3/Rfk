To look at this from a regulatory perspective, the "Generally Recognized As Safe" (GRAS) designation was established by Congress in the Food Additives Amendment of 1958. Its original intent was to exempt common, historically safe ingredients—like salt, vinegar, and baking soda—from the lengthy pre-market approval process required for new food additives. 

Over the decades, the GRAS process has evolved, allowing manufacturers to self-determine whether an ingredient is safe based on scientific consensus, sometimes without formal FDA review. This system has been a subject of ongoing debate:
* **Critics and consumer advocacy groups** argue that the loophole allows companies to introduce new chemicals into the food supply with insufficient independent oversight or public transparency.
* **Proponents and industry representatives** maintain that the process relies on rigorous scientific panels and allows for efficient innovation while keeping food production functional.
