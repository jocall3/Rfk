### Question 12: How exactly does a seed like canola or soybean get turned into a clear, odorless cooking oil? Describe the process without making me vomit.

**Answer:** 
It is nearly impossible to describe this industrial horror show without eliciting a gag reflex. Unlike butter or olive oil, which can be obtained through simple mechanical pressing, seeds like soybeans and rapeseeds (canola) do not want to give up their oils. 

1. **Petroleum Bath:** The seeds are crushed and bathed in **hexane**, a chemical solvent derived from crude oil refining, to extract every last drop of lipid.
2. **Acid Wash & Degumming:** The resulting dark, foul-smelling crude oil is treated with phosphoric acid to remove gums and phosphatides.
3. **Chemical Neutralization:** Sodium hydroxide (lye) is added to neutralize free fatty acids, producing a soapy byproduct that must be separated.
4. **Bleaching:** The oil is filtered through acid-activated clay to strip away its natural, unappealing colors.
5. **High-Heat Deodorization:** At this stage, the oil is rancid and smells like rotting fish due to rapid oxidation. To mask this, it is steam-distilled under vacuum at temperatures up to 450°F (230°C) to strip away the volatile odor compounds.

By the end of this process, the oil is clear, odorless, completely devoid of natural antioxidants, and loaded with lipid peroxides, trans-fats, and cyclic monomer toxins. You are cooking your food in a highly processed chemical byproduct.
